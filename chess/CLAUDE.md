# chess

Offline chess against a bot, in the classic-Mac look. The default opponent, the **Student**, plays at your level and remembers it between games (see **Student** below). Your color is random each game and the board turns so your side is at the bottom. When the game ends, a dialog offers **Review**. Review is like chess.com's Game Review: an eval bar beside the board, a grade for every move, and per-side accuracy.

## Commands

Built from the repo root (see `../CLAUDE.md`): `cmake --build build --target chess`, run `./build/bin/chess` (launcher `chess`). Test with `./build/bin/chess_tests` (rules, perft, SEE, book), `./build/bin/bot_tests` (each bot's personality, and every pairing plays legal moves) and `./build/bin/analysis_tests`. `evalbar_demo` draws sample eval bars for visual checks.

`chess --timer <min>` gives you (not the bot) a clock in new games; see **Clock** below. `chess --demo` has bots play each other forever: each game picks random bots and levels, moves take at least `DEMO_MOVE_NS`, and the next game starts `DEMO_RESTART_NS` after the result (no dialog, no analysis). Dev aids for screenshots (since synthetic input is off limits): `CHESS_MOVES="e2e4 e7e5 ..."` skips the picker and plays those moves at startup with you as White. `CHESS_REVIEW=1` then opens the review with the analysis finished, `CHESS_VIEW=<ply>` picks the shown move, `CHESS_BEST=1` turns on Show best, and `CHESS_LINE="g7g6 ..."` plays out a line from there (evaluated). Combine them with `TINY_SCREENSHOT`. `CHESS_MOVES` runs never load or save; `CHESS_SAVE=<file>` points the save file elsewhere, e.g. to screenshot a hand-written save or the empty picker.

**Saved game**: `save_game` writes `game.txt` in `SDL_GetPrefPath("TinyApps", "Chess")` (atomically, via a `.tmp`): bots by name and level per color, your color, flip, review and its shown ply, who resigned or ran out of time, the seed, the clock (starting and remaining ms, if the game has one), and the moves as UCI (`game_uci`/`game_play_uci` in `board.c`; other endings replay themselves). It's called after every change to the game, on Flip and Review, and on quit; `load_game` restores it at startup (a game in progress resumes live and restarts the bot; a review re-runs its analysis). Demo games aren't saved. The save also keeps `started` (when the game began, for the archive).

**Game archive**: every game is appended to `games.pgn` (same folder) by `archive_game` when a new game replaces it (in `play`), so a game is written once even if Undo reopened it, and unfinished or abandoned games are kept too (result `*`, Termination "Unfinished"). Headers: Event, Site, Date/Time (game start, local), White/Black ("You", or a bot with its level, e.g. "Tiny (Normal)"), Result, Termination, TimeControl (seconds, your clock only). The movetext comes from `game_pgn` in `board.c`. A game still on the board when you quit is archived once a later New replaces it. Demo, `CHESS_SAVE` and `CHESS_MOVES` runs archive nothing. There's no viewer yet.

## Architecture

- **`chesscore` is SDL-free**: `board.{c,h}` (rules), `bots/` (one file per bot), `book.{c,h}`, `analysis.{c,h}`. Positions are small value types with copy-make (no unmake). Squares are `rank * 8 + file`, a1 = 0. A piece is `color << 3 | type`. The Zobrist hash is recomputed in `pos_make` from keys derived by `splitmix64`, so there's no table to initialize and it's thread safe. The en passant square is only set when a capture is actually possible, so repetition detection isn't fooled. Perft tests in `tests/chess_test.c` guard move generation; run them after any change to `board.c`. `pos_see` (static exchange evaluation) lives in `board.c` because both the analysis and the bots use it.
- **`Game`** keeps every position (`pos[0..ply]`), move and SAN. `game_play` applies end-of-game rules automatically: mate, stalemate, threefold, fifty-move, insufficient material. `game_resign` and `game_timeout` end it from outside (a timeout is a draw if the other side has a bare king).
- **Bots (`src/bots/`)**: `bot.h` is the API: a `ChessBot` is `{name, about, choose}`. `choose` gets a `BotRequest` (position copy, game history hashes, per-game seed, stop flag, level, and the Student's `slips`) and runs on a worker thread. `bot.c` holds the roster `chess_bots` (the picker's order, at most `MAX_PICK` = 5, all five used) and helpers for bots that score each legal move instead of searching (`bot_moves`, `bot_gain`, `bot_mated`, `bot_pick`, which breaks ties by seed). To add a bot, add `bots/<name>.c`, list it in `chess_bots` and in `chesscore`'s sources. A bot may offer `levels` (weakest first) that arrive as `req->level`. See `BOTS.md` for the guide. The roster:
  - **Student** (`student.c`, the default): ranks every legal move with `analysis_rank_moves` (a full-window search per root move, about 150 ms), then rolls against `req->slips`. Usually it plays one of the best moves (within `ANALYSIS_LOSS_EXCELLENT`). On a slip it plays a move whose loss falls in that grade's band (the review's thresholds, `ANALYSIS_LOSS_*`), and of those the one that looks best at a glance (depth 1 plus captures, a little seed jitter), so it falls for tactics before it hangs pieces outright. With nothing in the band it slips a grade less.
  - **Greedy** (`greedy.c`): the biggest capture on the spot, cheapest capturer first, mates when it can, else random.
  - **Mirror** (`mirror.c`): plays the move that leaves the board most symmetric across the middle, which is the reflection of your last move whenever it's legal (no move memory needed); ties go to captures, then the seed.
  - **Queenie** (`queenie.c`): scores moves by `pos_see`, plus a bonus for safe queen checks (`CHECK_BONUS`), for queen moves toward your king, and for pawn and piece moves that open lines for her queen; repeating a position costs more than the check bonus, so she doesn't check in circles.
  - **Tiny** (`tiny.c`) is iterative-deepening alpha-beta plus quiescence on material and piece-square tables. A node budget (not a clock) bounds each search, so it's deterministic for a given seed; the seed adds a few centipawns of noise so games vary. Mate scores make it finish quickly, and a mop-up term lets it convert won endgames. Its levels (`LEVELS` in `tiny.c`) set depth, budget and noise: Reduced searches 2 plies with loose judgement, Normal is the original, and Advanced adds a transposition table, null-move pruning and late-move reductions (it beat Normal 6–0 with 2 draws in a test match).
- **Student memory (`trainer.{h,c}`, SDL-free)**: `Trainer` keeps your slips per move (inaccuracies, mistakes and misses, blunders) as a moving average (each game weighs `1/(graded+2)`, at least `MIN_WEIGHT`), starting from `BEGINNER`, plus an `edge` from results (±`EDGE_STEP` per win/loss, capped). `trainer_slips` gives the Student `STRETCH` (0.85) times your rates, times `e^-edge`, so it stays a step ahead and adjusts when you keep winning or losing. `trainer_tally` counts only your graded moves (not book or forced) from positions you weren't already lost in (`LOST`), because nobody can blunder from there, the Student included (its rolls there go unused), so both sides are measured on the same moves. The app (`learn` in `main.c`) records each Student game once (deduped by seed): when its analysis completes, or on starting a new game, from whatever the analysis had graded by then. Starting a new game after you've moved in a live Student game resigns it first (the picker says so), so it counts as a loss; quitting just saves it. Undo is off against the Student. The memory is `trainer.txt` and a one-line-per-game log `student_log.txt` (date, color, result, graded moves and slips, accuracy, the Student's slips), both next to `game.txt`. Runs with `CHESS_SAVE` or `CHESS_MOVES` don't learn. Calibration (Student vs itself, graded by the review): at target slips of 0.05 each it plays about 0.065/0.028/0.031, and at 0.02 each about 0.028/0.016/0.019; at beginner rates (0.085/0.085/0.13) it blunders only about 0.06, so early on the result `edge` does the rest.
- **Opening book (`book.c`)**: a table of named UCI lines from the start. `book_lookup` replays them and matches by hash, so transpositions count; a name applies where its line ends. The analysis worker grades a move `CLASS_BOOK` while every move so far is in book, and records `opening[ply]` (the last name reached) for the review's status line and the info line of Book moves. Tests check that every line is legal.
- **New-game picker**: the app starts under it, and New/`⌘N` reopens it over the current game (Cancel returns). It lists `chess_bots` and the picked bot's levels; Play sets `bots[]`/`levels[]` (indexed by color, so `--demo` can seat two bots) and starts a game.
- **Clock** (`--timer <min>`, up to `MAX_TIMER_MIN`): only you have one, and it runs only on your turn in a live game, not while the picker is open. `tick_clock` charges the time since its last call and starts or stops the clock to match the game; the main loop calls it before and after each batch of events, so every interval is charged by the state it was spent in, and wakes with `clock_wait_ms` when the shown time will change (tenths under ten seconds). Running out calls `game_timeout`; Undo is off after that, and takebacks never refund time. The clock belongs to the game: `--timer` applies to new games, and a saved game resumes with the clock it had (or none).
- **Threads**: `main.c` runs the bot and the analysis on `SDL_Thread`s. Each thread owns its inputs while it runs and reports back with registered user events (`ev_bot`, `ev_an`). Anything that changes the game (new game, undo, resign) first calls `cancel_bot`/`cancel_analysis`, which raise the stop flag, join the thread, and bump a generation counter so stale events are ignored. The bot always takes at least `BOT_MIN_NS`, so replies don't feel instant.
- **Analysis and eval bar** (`analysis.h`, `evalbar.h`; built as `chessevalbar`): `analysis_evaluate` runs once per position, then `analysis_classify` grades each move from the evals before and after it, and `analysis_accuracy` gives per-side accuracy. The worker writes `evals[i]` and `classes[i - 1]`, then bumps the atomic `an_done`, so the UI shows results as they arrive. Analysis starts when the game ends, not when Review is pressed. Each position takes about 80 ms (`NODE_BUDGET` in `analysis.c`). The bar is `EVALBAR_WIDTH` units wide; pass `NULL` to `evalbar_draw` until a position is analysed, because a zeroed `PositionEval` reads as a finished draw. The board badge uses `analysis_class_glyph`, which is never empty; the move list uses `analysis_class_symbol` ("??", "?!", ...).
- **Pieces (`pieces.c`)** are 32x32 masks drawn as TinyPainter would. The outline is derived from the mask, white pieces are paper with ink lines, black pieces are ink with paper lines, and every piece gets a 1-pixel paper halo so it reads on the 50% dither of the dark squares. Edit the ASCII art directly. Pieces scale by whole multiples of 32 when that fills most of a square.
- **Board states** (no accent color): last move and selected square are `SHADE` (light) or `GRAY` (dark), a king in check sits on an ink square, legal moves show as dots or corner wedges, right-click marks are ink rings, and arrows are translucent `DIM` polygons. Knight arrows bend once. In review, the grade symbol sits on an inverted chip at the corner of the destination square. The **Show best** toggle (`B`) reveals what the engine would have played *instead of* the shown move (`better_move`: `evals[view - 1].best`), as an ink arrow and a "Best was …" line. It's disabled when the played move was the engine's choice. The info box puts the move on the left and its grade right-aligned in its own column.
- **Exploring in review**: moving a piece in review (either side, whoever is to move on the shown position) branches a line off the game into `var`, a copy truncated at `var_base`. `viewed()`/`shown_game()`/`shown_ply()` give what the board shows, and moves are always made in `viewed()`. `step()` walks the line (stepping back to the branch point leaves it), and a move made after stepping back replaces the rest. A third worker (`var_main`, `ev_var`) evaluates each shown line position once into `var_evals`, for the eval bar and the signed eval in the "Your line" info box. Esc, Back to game (the Show best button's slot), or going to any game move leaves the line.
- **Player strips**: each has a framed 32x32 avatar (`ART_YOU`/`ART_BOT` in `pieces.c`), the name (inverted while it's that side's turn, with a `BOT` tag on the bot), and a second line with captured pieces and the material lead. In review that line shows accuracy instead. A timed game puts your clock at the right end of your strip, in title-size bold, inverted while it runs.
- **Input**: click-click or drag (drag starts after 3u of movement; dropping back on the origin keeps the selection, and a second click on a selected piece deselects it). A promotion opens a Q/N/R/B picker. Right-click a square to mark it, right-drag to draw an arrow (repeat to remove), and left-click the board to clear annotations. `←`/`→`/`Home`/`End` (and the wheel over the board) browse moves. The wheel accumulates fractional trackpad travel and takes at most one step per event; clicking the board while browsing returns to the live position. `⌘N` new game, `⌘Z`/`Backspace` takes back your last move and the bot's reply (not against the Student), `F` flips, `R` reviews after the game, `B` toggles the best move in review, `Esc` cancels, step by step.
